\begin{frame}{Selection vs. Treatment}
\textbf{Example:} OSU students were only required to live on campus for one year. Then:\bsk
``OSU students who \uline{choose to live on campus their 2nd year}\\are more likely to \uline{have a good GPA}.''
\bsk
\textbf{Competing Explanations:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item<2-> Living on campus \textit{causes} higher GPAs
    \begin{itemize}
        \item<3-> Better study habits?
        \item<3-> Fewer distractions?
    \end{itemize}
    \item<4> Only nerds choose to live on campus a 2nd year!!
\end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Selection vs. Treatment}
    Imagine that ``living on campus'' is like a drug trial.
    \bsk
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Treatment:} The drug treatment actually works. ``Living on campus'' actually causes better GPAs.
        \item \textbf{Selection:} The drug treatment doesn't do anything! It's just that those who select into the drug trial are different than those that don't.
    \end{itemize}
    \bsk
    With this data alone, we can't know which is true (or both)
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Selection vs. Treatment}
More examples:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Headline:} People who \uline{learn debate skills} are more likely to \uline{have leadership roles}.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Treatment:} Debate helps you learn leadership skills.
        \item \textbf{Selection:} People who choose debate already have good leadership skills
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Headline:} People who \uline{drink in moderation} are more likely to \uline{live longer}.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Treatment:} Alcohol harms your health
        \item \textbf{Selection:} Homebodies don't drink much
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Headline:} People who \uline{live together before marriage} are more likely to \uline{divorce}.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Treatment:} Cohabitation harms relationship/commitment dynamics
        \item \textbf{Selection:} People who live together are less religious/more ``okay'' with divorce
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Selection vs. Treatment}
    ``Selection'' doesn't have to be voluntary.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Headline:} People who \uline{are from disadvantaged backgrounds} are more likely to \uline{have an undiagnosed eating disorder}.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{Treatment:} Poor $\Rightarrow$ stress $\Rightarrow$ eating disorder
            \item \textbf{Selection:} 
        \end{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Headline:} People who \uline{use PTO at work} are more likely to \uline{have high job satisfaction}.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{Treatment:} PTO $\uparrow$ job satisfaction
            \item \textbf{Selection:} If you have a great job, you have both PTO and satisfaction
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Selection vs. Treatment}
    Getting political:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Headline:} People who \uline{vote for ``my party''} are more likely to \uline{[do some really great thing]}.
        \item \textbf{Headline:} People who \uline{vote for ``the other party''} are more likely to \uline{[be horrible in some way]}.
    \end{itemize}
    \bsk
    Be very critical!
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Is that correlation even true?
        \item There are \textbf{tons} of possible selection effects:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Education
            \item Race
            \item Age
            \item Religion
            \item $\cdots$
        \end{itemize}
        ...and that makes it easy to find crazy correlations
    \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What's the Solution}
    What are the solutions?
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Random assignment!} Randomly assign $1/2$ of OSU 2nd years to the dorms, $1/2$ to off-campus apartments.\\
        Selection effects are impossible! Only treatment remains.
        \item If random assignment is impossible (eg, being poor), just be honest about the uncertainty.
    \end{enumerate}
    \bsk
    Growing distrust in both researchers and media...
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Read and decide for yourself
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
